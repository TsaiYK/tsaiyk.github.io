Teaching

Teaching Philosophy

Even in an era of powerful simulation tools and AI, I believe that a strong foundation in engineering fundamentals remains essential—not just to solve problems, but to critically evaluate results, understand the underlying physics, and explain why an answer makes sense.

Courses

MMIE 7312 Spring 2027

Digital Twins

Ph.D.-Level Course · Texas State University

This course introduces the foundations of digital twins for autonomous dynamic systems, with applications to robots and manufacturing systems.

  • Topics: probability and statistics, surrogate modeling (including system identification), machine learning and AI, optimization, control, and validation, verification, and uncertainty quantification (VVUQ).
  • Project: TBD
ME 4351 Fall 2026

Control Systems

Undergraduate Course · Texas State University

This course covers introductory concepts of linear control systems.

  • Topics: block diagrams, mathematical modeling of physical systems, the Laplace transform, transfer functions, state-space representation, transient and steady state system responses, and stability.
  • Project: Take an unstable inverted pendulum on a motor-driven cart from equations to a working controller: model it, analyze its stability, and design feedback controllers that keep it balanced.
MEEN 357

Engineering Analysis for Mechanical Engineers

Undergraduate Course · Texas A&M University

This course provides a practical foundation for the use of numerical methods to solve engineering problems.

  • Topics: solving systems of linear equations, curve fitting, root finding, numerical integration, numerical differentiation, initial value problems, boundary value problems, eigenvalues, and optimization methods.
  • Project: Apply numerical methods and Python programming to model, simulate, and optimize an autonomous Mars rover capable of navigating challenging Martian terrain.
